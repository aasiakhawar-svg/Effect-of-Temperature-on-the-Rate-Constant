1🏔️ The hill every reaction must climb

Scene 1 – Two particles meet. Do they react? Only if they arrive with enough energy.

Activation energy, EₐThe minimum energy colliding particles need so that the reaction can happen – the height of the hill.
Two things must be true① energy ≥ Eₐ ② correct orientation. Orientation is hidden inside the factor A.
Picture itPushing a ball over a hill: a gentle push rolls back; a hard push gets over and releases energy on the way down.
🎯 Question for the next scenes: what does heating change – the hill, or the pushes?

2🔥 Heating gives more molecules a hard enough push

Scene 2 – The Maxwell–Boltzmann curve (schematic). Drag temperature up and watch the orange tail beyond Eₐ grow.

Major effect ✔Fraction of molecules with E ≥ Eₐ rises sharply with T.
Minor effect ✘Collisions get a little more frequent – tiny compared with the first effect, so ignore it.

3🧩 Decode the Arrhenius equation

Scene 3 – One equation, four characters. Play with the numbers.

k = A e−Eₐ/RT
APre-exponential factor: collision rate and orientation.
e−Eₐ/RTFraction of collisions with E ≥ Eₐ.
R, TR = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. T in kelvin. Eₐ in joules!

Textbook check: Eₐ = 60 kJ mol⁻¹ at 298 K gives 3 × 10⁻¹¹ – only 1 collision in 3 × 10¹¹ reacts.

🧠 T↑ → Eₐ/RT↓ → exponent less negative → e−Eₐ/RT↑ → k↑ → rate↑

4📉 Turn the curve into a straight line

Scene 4 – Take natural logs: ln k = −(Eₐ/R)(1/T) + ln A. It’s y = mx + c! Watch the data from the worked example appear.

Gradient= −Eₐ/R = (−3.50 − −5.27) ÷ (0.00294 − 0.00333) = −4538 K
Eₐ= 4538 × 8.31 = 37 700 J mol⁻¹ = +37.7 kJ mol⁻¹
Intercepton the vertical axis = ln A
⚠️ Plot ln k (y) against 1/T (x). The line slopes DOWN because the gradient is −Eₐ/R. Assumes Eₐ and A stay constant over the range.

5🗺️ Reading reaction profiles

Scene 5 – Choose a mechanism and follow the ball.

Transition stateEnergy MAXIMUM, lasts ~10⁻¹⁵ s, not a real species.
IntermediateEnergy MINIMUM (e.g. carbocation R₃C⁺), exists for a finite time.
RDS = highest humpSN1 tertiary: step 1 is slow so Eₐ(1) > Eₐ(2). If step 2 were rate-determining, Eₐ(1) < Eₐ(2).

6🧪 Structure decides the route

Scene 6 – Alkyl groups push electrons (inductive effect), so tertiary carbocations are the most stable. Pick a halogenoalkane.

SN1: stable carbocation → lower Eₐ → rate 1° < 2° < 3°. SN2: crowded 5-group transition state → higher Eₐ → rate 3° < 2° < 1°. Secondary: a mixture of both.
📌 Learning tip: large Eₐ = slow reaction, but rate rises rapidly with temperature. Small Eₐ = fast, less temperature-sensitive. Catalysed reactions have small Eₐ.
